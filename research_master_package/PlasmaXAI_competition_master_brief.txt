PlasmaXAI Competition Master Brief
======================================================================

Title
----------------------------------------------------------------------
PlasmaXAI: A Counterfactual-Guided Explainable AI Framework and Doctor-
Facing Clinical Workspace for Malignant Plasma Cell Recognition in
Multiple Myeloma


Executive Summary
----------------------------------------------------------------------
PlasmaXAI is a research and deployment-ready framework designed to
support the recognition of malignant plasma cells in multiple myeloma
microscopy images. The project addresses a clinically meaningful
problem: manual plasma-cell review is time-consuming, expertise
dependent, and difficult to scale in settings where pathologists and
hematologists must screen large numbers of marrow smear images while
still preserving interpretability and documentation quality.

The work evolved through three stages. The first stage established a
baseline notebook workflow on the PCMMD dataset. The second stage
optimized the core classification performance using hybrid deep-learning
and morphology-based benchmarking. The third and final stage introduced
the current PlasmaXAI framework, which is the main competition
submission. In this final stage, counterfactual morphology information is
not treated as a cosmetic post-hoc explanation; instead, it is embedded
inside a learned fusion module that influences the final classification
decision. This is one of the strongest originality points of the
project.

At the final preferred operating point, PlasmaXAI achieved 93.42%
accuracy, 93.43% weighted F1, 91.24% plasma precision, 94.63% plasma
recall, and 97.76% AUC on the held-out test set. It outperformed the
main deep and morphology-based baselines on the most practically
important metrics for a clinical screening scenario. In addition to the
model, a doctor-facing website was built to show how the research can be
translated into an interactive clinical review workflow with accounts,
patient history, image review, explainability, case notes, and
downloadable reports.


1. Clarity of Problem and Research Objective
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Multiple myeloma is a hematologic malignancy defined by abnormal plasma-
cell proliferation in the bone marrow. Morphologic review remains a key
part of diagnosis, disease monitoring, and treatment planning. In many
settings, the pathologist or hematologist must manually inspect marrow
smears or plasma-cell images to identify suspicious or malignant
patterns. This manual process has several practical weaknesses.

First, it is labor intensive. Even experienced reviewers must spend time
inspecting image after image, especially when the workflow involves
screening support, disease follow-up, or second-review documentation.
Second, the process is reader dependent. Interpretation can be affected
by experience level, fatigue, stain variability, and subtle morphologic
ambiguity. Third, the workflow is difficult to scale in lower-resource
or high-volume environments, where specialist availability may be
limited. Finally, most AI tools in digital pathology still emphasize raw
performance more than usable interpretation, which makes them less
suitable for clinician-in-the-loop deployment.

PlasmaXAI was developed to address exactly that gap. The central problem
statement of this project is:

"Can we build an explainable, high-performing plasma-cell recognition
system that not only improves diagnostic support on microscopy images,
but also presents its evidence in a way that is useful to doctors rather
than only to ML developers?"

The research objectives were therefore defined as follows:

1. Build a strong malignant plasma-cell recognition framework on PCMMD.
2. Improve over benchmark CNN and morphology-only baselines.
3. Move explainability into the decision pathway instead of attaching it
   only after classification.
4. Add patient-level consistency analysis to explore whether malignant
   signals are coherent across grouped cases.
5. Package the research in a clinically meaningful and deployment-aware
   way through a website and report system.

The scope of the work is therefore broader than a single classifier. It
includes dataset usage, benchmarking, learned fusion, counterfactual
reasoning, reliability analysis, patient aggregation, report generation,
and interface design for doctor workflows.


2. Methodology and Technical Approach
----------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Dataset and Split Strategy
The project used the PCMMD dataset, a dataset of plasma-cell images
designed to support multiple myeloma diagnosis. PCMMD Set 1 was used for
cell-level model development and benchmarking, while PCMMD Set 2 was
used for exploratory patient-level consistency analysis. The main split
strategy for Set 1 was stratified 70/15/15 for training, validation, and
testing respectively. In the notebook execution, this corresponded to
2482 training images, 532 validation images, and 532 test images.

2.2 Feature Design
PlasmaXAI uses both deep image information and explicit morphology
signals.

Image pathway:
- ResNet50 embeddings
- DenseNet121 embeddings

Morphology pathway:
- nucleus-to-cytoplasm ratio
- nucleus area
- cytoplasm area
- staining intensity
- granularity
- roundness
- mean RGB features
- related transformed ratios

The image encoders capture rich visual texture and shape patterns, while
the morphology branch preserves clinically interpretable descriptors.
This dual representation is important because plasma-cell malignancy is
not only a generic image-recognition problem; it is also a morphology-
driven problem.

2.3 Counterfactual-Guided Fusion
The most original methodological contribution is the way counterfactual
information is used. Instead of generating explanations only after the
prediction, PlasmaXAI trains a morphology-based counterfactual boundary
model that estimates how far a sample lies from a benign decision region
and which feature shifts would move it toward that region. These
counterfactual signals are then fed into the learned fusion network
itself.

The final fusion framework includes:
- ResNet50 branch
- DenseNet121 branch
- morphology branch
- counterfactual branch
- score/decision branch

These are combined using:
- learned encoders per modality
- sigmoid gates that modulate image streams using counterfactual signals
- a softmax modality gate for final weighted fusion

This design makes the explainability pathway structurally relevant to the
prediction, which is stronger than common post-hoc visualization
approaches.

2.4 Optimization, Thresholding, and Evaluation
Training used CUDA, AdamW-style optimization logic, validation-based
selection, and threshold tuning. Multiple systems were compared,
including:
- ResNet50
- DenseNet121
- previous optimized hybrid
- morphology logistic regression
- morphology RBF-SVM
- morphology random forest
- morphology XGBoost

The final preferred operating mode for competition reporting is the
precision-recall balanced validation plateau, with threshold 0.72. This
operating point was selected because it produced the best practical
balance across the doctor-relevant metrics rather than only maximizing a
single score.

2.5 Reliability and Statistical Support
The methodology did not stop at a single held-out test result. Additional
analysis included:
- ablation study
- bootstrap confidence intervals
- calibration/bootstrap diagram
- exact permutation testing for patient-level exploratory separation
- leave-one-patient-out patient-level evaluation

This is important for competition judging because it shows that the
implementation logic is not superficial. The work includes performance,
reliability, and interpretability evidence.

2.6 Doctor-Facing Translation
A major technical strength of this project is that the research was
translated into a website rather than left as a notebook-only prototype.
The website includes:
- doctor login and account flow
- dashboard
- new case upload
- case review workspace
- patient directory and patient profile pages
- report viewing
- image review
- doctor notes
- patient CRUD in the local workspace

This demonstrates feasibility beyond experimentation and supports the
real-world application score.


3. Originality and Innovation
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The strongest originality claim is not that PlasmaXAI uses one totally
unknown neural block. The novelty is system-level and methodological:

1. Counterfactual guidance is embedded inside the decision path.
   Many systems show explanations after prediction. PlasmaXAI uses
   counterfactual morphology information as an active input to the fusion
   process itself.

2. The model is not a fixed weighted ensemble.
   The earlier optimization stage used weighted blending. The final
   framework replaced that with a learned counterfactual-guided fusion
   module.

3. The framework combines image, morphology, and counterfactual evidence.
   This is more clinically meaningful than relying on CNN outputs alone.

4. The project extends from cell-level recognition to patient-level
   consistency analysis.
   That adds an additional interpretive layer beyond single-image
   classification.

5. The work is paired with a doctor-facing clinical workspace.
   Instead of stopping at a model checkpoint, the project shows how
   explainable digital pathology can be turned into a usable review
   system.

So the originality claim should be stated honestly but strongly:
PlasmaXAI is novel as an explainable clinical decision-support framework
that fuses deep image features, morphology, and counterfactual signals
in one learned architecture, rather than as a trivial UI wrapped around a
standard CNN.


4. Results and Expected Outcomes
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The final preferred test metrics for PlasmaXAI are:
- Accuracy: 93.42%
- Weighted F1: 93.43%
- Plasma precision: 91.24%
- Plasma recall: 94.63%
- AUC: 97.76%

These numbers are strong not only in isolation but also relative to the
baselines:
- ResNet50: 91.92% accuracy, 89.02% precision, 93.80% recall, 97.05% AUC
- Prev Hybrid: 91.73% accuracy, 90.91% precision, 90.91% recall, 97.12% AUC
- DenseNet121: 89.85% accuracy, 90.17% precision, 87.19% recall, 96.36% AUC

PlasmaXAI therefore leads the main benchmark on accuracy, plasma
precision, and plasma recall simultaneously. This is especially
important for screening support, where recall matters for suspicious-cell
capture but precision also matters to reduce unnecessary downstream
review burden.

Bootstrap support at the selected operating point:
- Accuracy 95% CI: 91.16 to 95.30
- Weighted F1 95% CI: 91.17 to 95.31
- Plasma recall 95% CI: 91.30 to 97.20
- AUC 95% CI: 96.56 to 98.63

Ablation highlights:
- Removing morphology reduced performance
- Removing the counterfactual path caused the strongest recall drop
- This supports the design rationale of the full fusion framework

Patient-level exploratory findings:
- 10 total patients
- observed patient-level AUC: 1.000
- permutation p-values: 0.0040 one-sided, 0.0079 two-sided
- leave-one-patient-out AUC mean: 1.000
- dominant separating features: mean_r, mean_g, mean_b

These patient-level outcomes are promising but are reported as
exploratory because the cohort is small and internally sourced. That
honesty is important in a competition setting because it shows maturity
of interpretation rather than overclaiming.

Expected outcomes beyond the current numbers:
- stronger doctor trust through built-in explainability
- structured report generation
- scalable screening support workflow
- easier transition to prospective validation


5. Impact and Real-World Application
----------------------------------------------------------------------
PlasmaXAI is highly relevant to real-world hematopathology and digital
healthcare workflows.

5.1 Clinical relevance
Doctors reviewing plasma-cell morphology need:
- fast triage support
- consistent documentation
- confidence-aware interpretation
- access to prior case history
- transparent supporting evidence

PlasmaXAI addresses each of these through a combined research and
software package.

5.2 Societal and healthcare impact
In many healthcare environments, expert pathology time is limited.
Systems that improve throughput without hiding their reasoning can help:
- reduce repetitive review burden
- support earlier triage of suspicious cases
- improve consistency of documentation
- make computational pathology more accessible in lower-resource settings

5.3 Industry and translational impact
This work is also relevant to:
- digital pathology tooling
- hospital AI decision-support platforms
- explainable medical AI research
- diagnostic workflow software
- deployable clinician-in-the-loop review products

5.4 Scalability
The project already includes a web workspace and a separate inference
service concept. That means the work is not trapped in notebook form. It
can scale from:
- local review and competition demonstration
to
- hosted clinical review prototypes
to
- future production-grade systems with stronger backend integration

5.5 Sustainability and efficiency
The framework can support sustainability indirectly by:
- reducing repeated manual review effort
- improving triage efficiency
- lowering the cost of iterative review workflows
- supporting structured digital records and reusable case history
